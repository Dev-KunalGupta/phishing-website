Phishing Awareness

Phishing has evolved into a global epidemic, with attackers now deploying over 3.4 billion malicious emails every single day. Recent data from 2025 shows a staggering 3.8 million reported phishing attacks annually, a volume driven largely by the integration of generative AI, which has boosted the effectiveness of fraudulent messages by over 400%. These attacks are no longer just simple emails; they have diversified into "smishing" (SMS), "vishing" (voice), and "quishing" (malicious QR codes), targeting everything from social media credentials to corporate financial data.
In 2025, global phishing activity remains at a historically high "plateau," with approximately 3.8 million reported phishing attacks occurring throughout the year. On a daily basis, it is estimated that 3.4 billion malicious emails (including phishing, spam, and bulk) are sent worldwide.
Recent Global Case Numbers According to quarterly data from the Anti-Phishing Working Group (APWG), the number of unique phishing attacks (sites) has consistently hovered near or above one million per quarter.

What is Phishing?

“Phishing” refers to an attempt to steal sensitive information, typically in the form of usernames, passwords, credit card numbers, bank account information, or other important data in order to utilize or sell the stolen information. By masquerading as a reputable source with an enticing request, an attacker lures in the victim in order to trick them, similar to how a fisherman uses bait to catch a fish.
Phishing is a social engineering cyberattack where attackers impersonate legitimate entities via email, text, or calls to deceive individuals into revealing sensitive information like passwords, credit card numbers, or installing malware.
Phishing is a cybercrime where attackers impersonate trusted entities via email, text, or calls to deceive individuals into revealing sensitive information like passwords or financial data. These attacks often create urgency or fear to manipulate users.

Common Phishing Types

Email Phishing

This remains the #1 delivery method for malware, accounting for 91% of all cyberattacks. In 2024, the average person received roughly 14 malicious emails per month. A major shift in this category is the use of AI-enhanced language, which has eliminated the classic "poor grammar" red flag, making these emails nearly indistinguishable from official corporate communications.

QR Phishing (Quishing)

Quishing saw a 427% increase in 2024–2025 because QR codes bypass traditional email security filters that scan text and links. Attackers often place physical stickers over legitimate QR codes on parking meters, restaurant menus, or charging stations. Once scanned, they lead to "CSS-based" fake login screens designed specifically to steal mobile session tokens.

URL Spoofing (Typosquatting)

Beyond just swapping letters (like g00gle.com), attackers now use "homograph attacks" using look-alike characters from different alphabets (e.g., a Cyrillic "а" instead of a Latin "a"). Research shows that users are 70% more likely to click a spoofed URL if it includes a familiar brand name followed by a trusted keyword like -support.com or -verify.net.

How Phishing Attacks Work

Attackers create fake websites or messages that mimic trusted platforms. Users click links and enter sensitive data, which gets captured by attackers.

Real Phishing Examples

Fake: http://paytm-login-security.com

Issue: Extra words + HTTP

Real: https://paytm.com

Fake: http://amaz0n-support.net

Issue: '0' instead of 'o'

Real: https://amazon.in

How to Identify Phishing

  • Check domain spelling carefully
  • Avoid unknown links
  • Look for HTTPS
  • Beware of urgency
  • Verify sender identity
